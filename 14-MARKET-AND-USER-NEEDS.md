# Market and User Needs

## Working market hypothesis
Users and organizations do not primarily need more standalone agents.
They need reliable systems that turn intent into persistent, governed and verifiable outcomes while minimizing friction, integration complexity, risk and cost.

## Human hierarchy of needs
1. Outcome — get useful work done.
2. Ease — minimal setup.
3. Reliability — correct result.
4. Control — stop/approve/override.
5. Transparency — know what matters.
6. Economics — cost visibility and ROI.
7. Continuity — persistent work across sessions.
8. Portability — avoid vendor lock-in.
9. Improvement — learn preferences and patterns.
10. Ecosystem — use existing tools/systems.

## Enterprise demand themes
- ROI / measurable value
- integration
- governance
- security/privacy
- cost control
- auditability
- human-agent collaboration
- workflow redesign
- interoperability
- durable state

## Developer demand themes
- preserve flow
- intent-driven delegation
- inspectable diffs/results
- tests and verification
- quick takeover
- compatibility with existing repo/IDE/terminal workflows

## Positioning to avoid
Do not lead with:
“multi-agent orchestration platform”
“MCP manager”
“AI governance platform”
“prompt engineering 2.0”

Those describe implementation layers.

## Candidate value proposition
Tell the system the outcome you want. It coordinates the intelligence, tools and work needed to achieve it while keeping you in control of cost, risk and final decisions.
